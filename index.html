📜 Roman Terminology

Ludus

Literally meaning "game," "play," or "training," a ludus was an official training school where gladiators were housed, drilled, and conditioned under strict discipline.

Lanista

A private entrepreneur, broker, and owner of a gladiator troupe (familia gladiatoria). A lanista bought prisoners of war, slaves, or criminals, trained them, and rented them out to magistrates (editores) staging public games for profit. Because of their trade, they were viewed with social contempt (infamia) in Roman society, despite often acquiring immense wealth.

Ludus Magnus

Translating to the "Great Ludus," this was the largest of the four official gladiator training schools in Rome. Built by Emperor Domitian near the Colosseum, it featured its own miniature amphitheater and training arena connected directly to the Colosseum by an underground tunnel.
